\abstract
% context
{A star observed at very low Galactic rest-frame speed is caught close to
an orbital turning point, and its specific angular momentum is bounded
above by the product of its Galactocentric radius and its observed speed.
In a static, centrally concentrated potential its apocentre therefore lies
at or just beyond its present radius, making the low-\vgrf{} state a
kinematic selection with a largely predictable orbital corollary.  Gaia-era
catalogues selected at the opposite, high-speed extreme are numerous,
whereas the slow tail has not previously been surveyed systematically on
total rest-frame speed.}
% aims
{We aim to construct and publicly release a probabilistic Gaia DR3
catalogue of stars consistent with the low-\vgrf{} condition and to
separate the model-robust from the model-sensitive components of its
orbital characterisation.}
% methods
{Monte Carlo propagation of the full Gaia parallax--proper-motion
covariance, with Lindegren et al.\ (2021) parallax zero-points and
Bailer-Jones et al.\ (2021) photogeometric distances, yields per-star
membership probabilities for $\vgrf<25\kms$.  Orbits are integrated in
the axisymmetrized Hunter et al.\ (2024) potential and stress-tested
against barred, halo-mass, second-potential, and Solar-parameter
variants; observability-matched Gaia control samples at higher \vgrf{}
provide an empirical null.}
% results
{Membership probabilities are computed both as forward Monte Carlo scores
and, because the parent population rises steeply with \vgrf{}, after
reconstructing the latent \vgrf{} distribution; the latter correction
lowers the score-implied purity of a forward-defined $P>0.50$ sample from
$73\%$ to $42\%$, and we therefore define the catalogue on the
population-prior probability.  It comprises a primary Tier~A+B sample of
276 stars ($P>0.84$, purity $95\%$) within a broader Tier~A+B+C sample of
621 ($P>0.50$, $79\%$); these are strict nested subsets of the 541 and
1{,}952 stars selected by the forward scores, which are also released.
The sample is strongly radial, with compact cylindrical pericentres
(Monte Carlo posterior-median eccentricity 0.965, median $\Rperi=153$\,pc
and $\Rapo=8.24$\kpc), distinctly more so than the
matched controls.  The matched comparison is used as a null check that
the slow state is not a generic property of Gaia 6D stars, not as a
calibrated speed--eccentricity relation.}
% conclusions
{We release the tiered catalogue, the per-star Monte Carlo and orbit
products, and subset-qualified chemistry; completeness is set by the
sparse Gaia RVS selection function, not by the velocity cut.}

\section{Introduction} \label{sec:intro}
% =============================================================================

Gaia DR3 delivers full six-dimensional phase-space coordinates for tens
of millions of stars by combining its astrometry with radial velocities
from the RVS instrument \citep{Vallenari2023,Katz2023}.  Most Gaia-era
Galactic archaeology uses that information the same way: identify a
population---a disc component, a halo substructure, a stream of accreted
debris---and then characterise its kinematics
\citep[see][for reviews]{BinneyMerrifield1998,BlandHawthorn2016,
Helmi2020,Deason2024}.  The same data support a complementary approach:
select directly on a rare kinematic state, and ask afterwards what kinds
of star occupy it.  This paper takes that approach for one such state,
stars observed with unusually low instantaneous speed in the Galactic
rest frame (\vgrf).

Selection at a kinematic extreme is already well established at the fast
end.  Gaia DR3 has been searched for hypervelocity and high-velocity
candidates \citep{Marchetti2022,CarballoBello2025} and for halo samples
defined by high tangential velocity or reduced proper motion
\citep{Viswanathan2023}, and the fast tail has long been used to
constrain the Galactic escape speed and the mass of the Milky Way
\citep{Smith2007,Piffl2014,KoppelmanHelmi2021}.  The slow end has
attracted much less systematic attention.  The closest Gaia-era
counterpart to the present work is \citet{Filion2025}, who identify
$\sim 70$ stars with low or retrograde Galactocentric azimuthal velocity
and thin-disc-like abundances.  That selection is made on a single
velocity component within a chemical box; ours is made on the full
rest-frame speed and applies no chemical condition, so the two samples
are complementary rather than competing---we compare them directly in
Appendix~\ref{app:filion}.  We are not aware of an existing Gaia-era
catalogue selected on total rest-frame speed at this extreme, and
assembling one is the purpose of this paper.

A related motivation comes from empirical stellar-tracer approaches to
the local dark-matter velocity distribution, which use metal-poor and
accreted populations as observable proxies for the accreted dark matter
\citep{HerzogArbeitman2018PRL,HerzogArbeitman2018JCAP,
Necib2019Disequilibrium,Necib2019Fire,Evans2019SHM}; the low-speed part
of that distribution is of particular interest because solar
gravitational focusing acts most strongly on slow particles
\citep{Lee2014GF}.  We make no dark-matter inference here, and our
selection function is set by Gaia RVS rather than by any halo-tracer
criterion, so the catalogue is context for that literature rather than a
constraint on it.

The low-speed state is worth isolating because it carries a dynamical
implication that a single epoch of data can support.  A star observed
almost at rest with respect to the Galactic centre is, at that instant,
close to a turning point of its orbit: in a static, centrally
concentrated potential its apocentric radius lies at or just beyond its
present Galactocentric radius.  Its specific angular momentum is
likewise bounded above by the product of its Galactocentric radius and
its observed speed, which for a star near the solar radius moving at
$<25\kms$ is under $11\%$ of the circular-orbit value at the same
radius.  Both statements follow from the observed velocity vector alone
and are insensitive to the choice of potential.

What the low speed does \emph{not} fix is the depth of the inner turning
point.  Because cylindrical pericentre is controlled by the small
residual angular momentum, orbits consistent with the same low speed
span orders of magnitude in cylindrical pericentric distance---from the
kiloparsec-scale pericentres typical of radial accretion debris in the
apocentre-pile-up picture \citep{Deason2018} to very small cylindrical
pericentres for the lowest angular momenta.  A small cylindrical
pericentre by itself establishes only proximity to the Galactic spin
axis; a parsec-scale passage near Sgr~A* additionally requires small
$|z|$, and is assessed separately below using the three-dimensional
closest approach.  In either case the star crosses the inner Galaxy
quickly and spends most of each radial period near apocentre; what
distinguishes the configurations is how closely the orbit approaches the
Galactic centre.  Quantifying that closest approach star by star, from
the full velocity vector and its uncertainties, is what the orbit
modelling below sets out to do.

It is worth being explicit about the geometry this implies, because a
small pericentre is easy to picture as a comet-like turning point at
which the star reverses and retraces its path.  That is not what these
orbits do.  For a closed Kepler ellipse the azimuth swept between
successive apocentres is $360\degr$, so the star does return along its
inbound track; for a harmonic, constant-density core it is $180\degr$,
so successive apocentres lie on opposite sides of the centre.  In the
adopted potential the catalogue sits close to the latter: the median
azimuth swept per radial period is $195\degr$ (16th--84th percentile
$155\degr$--$202\degr$), and no catalogue member exceeds $300\degr$
(Section~\ref{sec:orbits}).  These stars therefore transit the inner
Galaxy and emerge on the far side, rather than reaching inward and
turning back.

The sample is therefore defined by present-day kinematics, not by
inferred origin.  We construct a probabilistic low-\vgrf{} Gaia DR3
catalogue, quantify how stable velocity-threshold membership is under
measurement uncertainties, and report model-dependent orbit diagnostics
in an adopted Milky Way potential.  The orbit integrations interpret the
observed slow state; they do not define the catalogue.  We compare the
low-\vgrf{} sample against matched Gaia DR3 control samples drawn from
higher velocity bands, and we treat external spectroscopic matches as
context for the subset with available chemistry rather than as a
population assignment for the catalogue as a whole.

Because this paper layers several kinds of statement, we distinguish them
explicitly at the outset.  \emph{Direct Gaia observables}---sky position,
parallax, proper motion, radial velocity, and the derived $\vgrf$---define
the catalogue.  \emph{Threshold-probability outputs}---the per-star
$P(\vgrf<25\kms)$ and the resulting tiers---are Monte Carlo membership
scores computed from those observables and their uncertainties.
\emph{Orbit-model outputs}---eccentricity, $\Rperi$, $\Rapo$, $\zmax$,
energy, angular momentum, and actions---are deterministic functions of an
adopted Galactic potential, and are model-dependent rather than measured.
\emph{Exploratory sensitivity products}---barred-potential central
reaches, long-apocentre inner-reach and Sgr~A* counts, resonance overlays, and chaos
diagnostics---probe robustness and are labelled as such throughout.  The
conclusions rest most heavily on the first two layers.  When both
point-estimate and Monte Carlo orbit products are quoted, the sampling
scheme is labelled explicitly.  The orbit-model layer also has a time
horizon: the direction of a star's next excursion follows from its
measured velocity, but an integration carried far enough forward or
backward describes the adopted potential rather than the star.  We set
that horizon quantitatively in Section~\ref{sec:interpretation} and
confine per-star trajectory statements to within it.

Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the sample definition, uncertainty
propagation, controls, and adopted orbit model.
Section~\ref{sec:empirical} presents the empirical catalogue properties
and chemical cross-matches.  Section~\ref{sec:orbits} gives the
model-dependent dynamical results.
Section~\ref{sec:interpretation} discusses the interpretation and its
limits, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} summarises the conclusions.

\section{Sample Definition and Methods} \label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Gaia DR3 Sample} \label{sec:gaia_sample}

The parent source is Gaia DR3 \citep{Vallenari2023}, restricted to stars
with full six-dimensional phase-space information: sky position,
parallax, proper motion, and radial velocity.  Radial velocities in DR3
are available for approximately 33.8 million stars observed by the
Radial Velocity Spectrometer \citep{Katz2023}.  This 6D subset has a
non-trivial selection function in sky position, magnitude, and colour
\citep{CastroGinard2023, CantatGaudin2023}; it is not a neutral slice
of the Galaxy, and distant 6D samples are commonly giant-heavy, with
incompleteness expected for cool faint dwarfs.

The sample was constructed from a local mirror of the Gaia DR3 source
table using the columns needed for a six-dimensional solution:
\texttt{source\_id},
\texttt{ra}, \texttt{dec}, \texttt{parallax}, \texttt{pmra},
\texttt{pmdec}, \texttt{radial\_velocity}, the corresponding
uncertainties and astrometric correlations, broad-band photometry,
\texttt{ruwe}, and \texttt{duplicated\_source}.  The equivalent Gaia
Archive selection is
\texttt{radial\_velocity IS NOT NULL}, \texttt{parallax > 0},
\texttt{pmra IS NOT NULL}, \texttt{pmdec IS NOT NULL}, and
\texttt{parallax\_over\_error > 5}; the literal ADQL query is shipped as
\texttt{ADQL\_selection.sql}.  No RUWE or duplicated-source cut is
applied to define the point-estimate catalogue; those flags define the
Gold subset below.  Heliocentric observables are transformed into
Galactocentric coordinates and velocities, and Galactic rest-frame speeds
are computed.

To avoid imposing the final catalogue boundary during candidate
construction, we first scanned the full local Gaia DR3 source mirror and
retained an intentionally broad legacy buffer with preliminary
$\vgrf<200$\kms.  This buffer contains 5{,}867{,}654 unique Gaia source
IDs.  After the final parallax zero-point, photogeometric-distance, and
Solar-frame conventions are applied, 2{,}755 sources have corrected
point-estimate $\vgrf<25$\kms{} within the 20{,}829-source
near-threshold candidate pool.  Monte Carlo propagation of the
observables, followed by the population-prior correction of
Section~\ref{sec:latent}, then defines the catalogue tiers used
throughout the paper: Tier~A ($P>0.95$, 173 stars), Tier~A+B ($P>0.84$,
276 stars), and Tier~A+B+C ($P>0.50$, 621 stars).  The remaining
2{,}134 corrected point-estimate sources below 25\kms{} have $P\leq0.50$
and are retained as Tier~D rather than as part of the primary catalogue.
For continuity with the deposited catalogue we also release the
uncorrected forward scores, which select 289, 541, and 1{,}952 stars at
the same three thresholds; the population-prior tiers are strict nested
subsets of these.

This is not a volume-complete sample and it is not intended as a complete
inventory of all such stars in the Milky Way.  It is a Gaia DR3 6D
catalogue defined by a simple instantaneous kinematic state and reported
with explicit threshold-membership probabilities.

\subsection{Quality Control} \label{sec:quality}

Because the slowest stars are the most vulnerable to measurement error,
we report conservative high-quality subsets in addition to the tiered
catalogue, following the Gaia DR3 catalogue-validation guidance
\citep{Babusiaux2023}.  The Gold observational-quality subset is defined
inside Tier~A+B by
RUWE $<1.4$, an acceptable DR3 RVS quality flag, parallax
signal-to-noise $>10$, and fractional distance uncertainty
$\sigma_d/d<0.15$.  Dynamical robustness is reported separately in
Section~\ref{sec:robustness}, where additional orbit-derived criteria
are applied.

Operationally, ``acceptable DR3 RVS quality'' means the released
\texttt{rv\_quality} field is \texttt{ok}: the source is not flagged as
\texttt{poor} by either $p(\chi^2_{\rm RV})<0.01$ or
\texttt{rv\_expected\_sig\_to\_noise}$<2$, and is not flagged as
\texttt{marginal} by the large-amplitude/few-transit or faint
out-of-template-temperature screens defined in the public pipeline script.

Several later diagnostics necessarily use narrower subsets.  The
$\alpha$-classified spectroscopic subset is defined only by the
availability of APOGEE or GALAH \FeH{} and alpha-element abundances
within Tier~A+B+C; it is used for within-subset literature-region
comparisons, not for whole-catalogue population fractions.  The
selection-function inverse-weighted sums are likewise reported only as
observability context, because many slow stars occupy sparse
colour--magnitude cells where the Gaia DR3 RVS selection-function tool
returns its prior mean.  Table~\ref{tab:rv_quality_sensitivity} reports
how the primary counts respond to these conservative RVS-quality cuts.

\subsection{Velocity-Threshold Uncertainty} \label{sec:vgrf_mc}

The catalogue is defined by threshold-membership probability rather than
by a hard point estimate alone.  For every source in the propagated
candidate pool we draw Monte Carlo realisations of
$(\varpi,\mu_{\alpha *},\mu_\delta)$ from the Gaia parallax--proper-motion
covariance submatrix, holding sky position fixed because the positional
uncertainties are negligible for the velocity scale considered here.
We draw radial velocity independently from its quoted uncertainty, apply
the \citet{Lindegren2021} zero-point correction, and draw distance from
the asymmetric \citet{BailerJones2021} photogeometric posterior,
following the parallax-handling guidance of \citet{Luri2018}.  We then
recompute $\vgrf$ for each realisation using the same Solar parameters
and coordinate convention as the point-estimate catalogue.  This
explicit propagation is complementary to recent Gaia DR3 Cartesian
catalogue work that compares second-order and Monte Carlo covariance
propagation for the full RVS sample \citep{Zhang2025}.

Tier-fraction percentages reported below carry Beta$(k+\tfrac{1}{2},
n-k+\tfrac{1}{2})$ Jeffreys 68\% intervals \citep{Cameron2011};
integer-count diagnostics (e.g., long-apocentre inner-reach counts) carry
\citet{Gehrels1986} Poisson 1$\sigma$ bands.

The Monte Carlo scheme uses 500 baseline realisations per source,
refines stars in the transition band $0.30<P<0.70$ to 5,000
realisations, and assigns 10,000 realisations to stars whose
point-estimate $\vgrf$ lies within 2\kms{} of the threshold.  A separate
100-star convergence check compares 500 and 5,000 realisations.  In this
release, 1{,}623 stars receive the 5,000-realisation refinement and
1{,}261 receive the 10,000-realisation near-threshold refinement.  We use
the resulting $P(\vgrf<25\kms)$ values to define Tier~A ($P>0.95$),
Tier~B increment ($0.84<P\leq0.95$), Tier~C increment
($0.50<P\leq0.84$), and Tier~D (corrected point estimate below
25\kms{} but $P\leq0.50$).

Because every star carries a membership probability, the score-implied
number of contaminants in any tier follows directly as $\sum(1-P)$ over
its members.  Which probability is used matters a great deal, and we
therefore state both.  Under the \emph{forward} Monte Carlo scores the
tiers would appear very clean: $94\%$ purity for a 541-star Tier~A+B and
$73\%$ for a 1{,}952-star Tier~A+B+C.  Those figures are biased high,
because the forward score takes no account of how many stars are
available at each true speed to scatter into the window; evaluated under
the population prior of Section~\ref{sec:latent}, the same two samples
have purities of only $80\%$ and $42\%$.  We therefore define the tiers
on the population-prior probability throughout.  On that definition the
purities are $99.0\%$ for the 173-star Tier~A core, $95.4\%$ for the
276-star Tier~A+B primary catalogue, and $78.6\%$ for the 621-star
Tier~A+B+C extension, corresponding to $12.7$, $12.7$, and $133$
score-implied false positives respectively.  Tier~A+B remains the
recommended high-confidence working sample and Tier~A+B+C the
high-completeness extension.

The retiering does not reorder the catalogue.  No star enters any tier
that was not already in the corresponding forward-defined tier: the
population-prior tiers are strict nested subsets, with rank correlations
between the two scores of $\rho_{\rm S}=0.997$ and $\tau_{\rm K}=0.963$.
The correction moves the thresholds rather than the ranking, and the
released products carry both scores so that either definition can be
reproduced.

The Tier~A+B boundary is used for the primary catalogue because $P>0.84$
corresponds approximately to a one-sided one-sigma probability threshold:
these stars are more likely than not to remain below 25\kms{} even after
a one-sigma upward fluctuation in the propagated threshold variable.  The
three operating thresholds correspond to standard one-sided Gaussian-tail
levels: $P>0.95$ (Tier~A) is approximately a one-sided $1.6\sigma$
threshold, $P>0.84$ (Tier~A+B) is approximately a one-sided $1\sigma$
threshold, and $P>0.50$ (Tier~A+B+C) is the more-likely-than-not
boundary.  Tier~C is reported as the broader, lower-confidence catalogue
extension rather than as part of the conservative primary sample.  These
Gaussian-tail correspondences are interpretive labels for the operating
thresholds, not detection significances or empirical truth calibrations.
We therefore describe the summed $1-P$ values as nominal internal
score-implied contamination, and test that score
with both a Monte Carlo self-consistency check and a truth-labelled mock
diagnostic below.  Central 68\% intervals on the
primary numerical claims are collected in
Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty_ledger}.

Because the \citet{BailerJones2021} photogeometric posterior is itself
conditioned on Gaia astrometry, drawing the distance independently of
the astrometric covariance is an approximation.  Recent kinegeometric
distance work combines parallaxes and proper motions with an explicit
velocity prior \citep{BailerJones2023}; we do not adopt that posterior
for this velocity-selected catalogue because the velocity prior would be
entangled with the low-\vgrf{} quantity being measured.  The catalogue
therefore uses the \citet{BailerJones2021} posterior as an external
asymmetric distance prior only to define threshold-membership
probabilities, and treats distance-method and covariance-coupling tests
as sensitivity diagnostics rather than as a full joint posterior or as
part of the catalogue definition.

We quantified this approximation with a stress test over the entire
tier-sensitive band: all 3{,}342 candidates with adopted
$P(\vgrf<25\kms)\geq0.30$.  The test keeps the BJ split-normal distance
marginal but imposes a maximal Gaussian-copula anti-correlation between
the parallax residual and the distance quantile, recomputing the
independent- and coupled-distance probabilities at matched realisations
so that $\Delta P$ isolates the coupling.  The median $|\Delta P|$ is
0.009, the 84th percentile 0.021, the 95th 0.028, and the largest shift
0.055.  Across the band, 114 of 3{,}342 sources change tier label; the
full tier-label confusion matrix is given in
Table~\ref{tab:covariance_stress}.  The primary catalogue is robust to
this worst-case coupling: all 276 Tier~A+B members remain above
$P=0.84$ under both the independent and the copula-coupled treatment,
with a median $|\Delta P|$ of $0.001$ and a maximum of $0.023$ across
that sample---the population-prior thresholds sit far enough from the
boundary that distance-prior coupling cannot move them.  The largest shifts
occur for near-threshold sources, which the probabilistic tiering already
flags (Fig.~\ref{fig:joint_distance_test}).

As a column-level audit of distance-prior sensitivity, we also report the
Gaia parallax signal-to-noise distribution for the tiered catalogue.  In
the primary Tier~A+B sample, 19 of 276 stars (6.9\%) lie in the lower-SNR
$5<\varpi/\sigma_\varpi\leq10$ interval and 257 (93.1\%) have
$\varpi/\sigma_\varpi>10$; none have a BJ 68\% fractional distance
half-width above 0.15.  In the broader Tier~A+B+C extension, where the
lower-confidence Tier~C dominates, 69 of 621 stars (11.1\%) lie in
the $5$--$10$ interval, 552 (88.9\%) exceed 10, and none have
BJ fractional distance half-width above 0.15.  Thus the potentially
prior-sensitive distance regime is concentrated in the completeness
extension, not in the primary catalogue; the copula test above is our
stress bound on how this approximation affects threshold membership.

A separate Monte Carlo self-consistency reconstruction repeats the 500-realisation
baseline pass with an independent random seed, selects the
2{,}275 sources with baseline $0.30<P<0.70$, and compares deciles of

truth-calibration of the catalogue probabilities.  It is distinct from the
Gaia RVS selection-function systematic quantified separately in
Section~\ref{sec:interpretation}.

\subsection{Galactocentric Transformation and Supporting Orbit Model}
\label{sec:orbit_model}

For the Galactocentric transformation we adopt $\Rsun = 8.178$\kpc
\citep{GravityCollab2019}, a Solar height above the plane of 25\pc,
Solar peculiar motion $(U, V, W) = (11.1, 12.24, 7.25)$\kms{} from
\citet{Schonrich2010}, and $\Vcirc = 229.0$\kms at the Solar radius,
consistent with the \citet{Eilers2019} rotation-curve value
($v_c(R_0)=229.0\pm0.2$\kms) and with the axisymmetrised
\citet{Hunter2024} potential used for the orbit integrations.  The
coordinate transformation is performed with \texttt{astropy.coordinates}
\citep{AstropyCollaboration2013,AstropyCollaboration2018,
AstropyCollaboration2022} and the resulting Cartesian
Galactocentric phase-space vectors are passed to \texttt{agama}.  Three
additional Solar-parameter variants from the recent literature
\citep{GravityCollab2022,Schonrich2012,ReidBrunthaler2020} are used as
a sensitivity bracket in Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}
(Table~\ref{tab:solar_variants}).

We apply the per-star \citet{Lindegren2021} Gaia parallax zero-point
correction and use the \citet{BailerJones2021} photogeometric distance
posterior as the default distance estimator.  The inverse-parallax
distance, with and without the zero-point correction, is retained only as
a sensitivity diagnostic.  In the propagated candidate pool,
the adopted photogeometric-distance convention gives 2{,}755
point-estimate sources below the cut before probability-tiering.

The default supporting orbit model is the static, axisymmetrized form of
the analytic Milky Way potential of \citet{Hunter2024}.  The underlying
Hunter+2024 model is not Gaia-DR3-calibrated; it combines analytic
bulge/bar, nuclear, disc, and halo components constrained by
rotation-curve and gas-terminal-velocity data.  We use its
axisymmetrized form as the catalogue default because it keeps orbital
quantities such as $E$, $L_z$, and St\"ackel-fudge actions well-defined.
Orbit integration and action estimates use \texttt{agama}
\citep{Vasiliev2019}; actions are computed with the St\"ackel-fudge
 formalism \citep{Binney2012,Sanders2016}.  The orbit integrations are
 fully three-dimensional, evolving $(x,y,z,v_x,v_y,v_z)$.  Throughout,
 $R(t)=\sqrt{x(t)^2+y(t)^2}$ and the reported $\Rperi=\min_t R(t)$ and
 $\Rapo=\max_t R(t)$ are cylindrical radial turning points.  Thus
 $\Rperi$ measures closest approach to the Galactic spin axis, not
 necessarily to Sgr~A* as a point.  Proximity to the Galactic centre is
 assessed separately using the spherical closest approach
 $r_{\rm sph,min}=\min_t\sqrt{x(t)^2+y(t)^2+z(t)^2}$.
 Point-estimate integrations span 4\,Gyr, and cylindrical pericentres,
 apocentres, and maximum heights are read
from a 40{,}001-point, parabola-interpolated trajectory sampling; the
5{,}000-realisation orbit Monte Carlo uses the same 4\,Gyr interval with
1{,}001 stored samples per realisation.  This interval samples many
radial periods for the compact inner-reaching orbits ($T_r \lesssim
100$\,Myr at $\Rperi \sim 100$\,pc) while keeping the orbit products
descriptive rather than evolutionary.  A static-potential re-integration
of a random 100-star Tier~A subset to 8\,Gyr changes the sample medians
by only $+5.5$\,pc in $\Rperi$, $+0.017$\,kpc in $\Rapo$, and
$+0.039$\,kpc in $\zmax$ relative to the 4\,Gyr products.  This
longer-time experiment is a convergence check only; the released orbit
statistics use the full Tier~A+B+C integrations.  A rigidly
rotating barred Hunter/Sormani variant
\citep{Sormani2022} is used as a sensitivity test for non-axisymmetric
inner-Galaxy effects, not as the default catalogue definition.  The
static integrations conserve energy to a median relative drift of
$6\times10^{-7}$; in the rotating barred frame the inertial-frame energy
is not conserved (median relative drift $2\%$, 99th percentile $12\%$),
but the Jacobi integral $E_J=E-\Omega_p L_z$ is conserved to a median
$5\times10^{-7}$, confirming integration fidelity.

In this manuscript, orbit-derived quantities such as eccentricity,
$\Rperi$, $\Rapo$, $\zmax$, energy, angular momentum, and actions are
presented as outputs of the adopted model.  They are informative, but
 they remain model-dependent, especially for stars whose inferred
 cylindrical pericentres lie within a few tens of parsecs of the Galactic
 spin axis.
Pericentres, apocentres, and maximum heights are measured directly from
the integrated trajectories, not inferred from St\"ackel-fudge actions.
The reported single-evaluation actions are accompanied by a full-sample
convergent audit: for every Tier~A+B+C star they are compared with
orbit-time-averaged actions over the 4\,Gyr integration.  The azimuthal
action $J_\phi\equiv L_z$ is recovered exactly and $J_R$ to a median
$8\%$, but St\"ackel-fudge accuracy degrades for the vertical action
$J_z$ of these plunging orbits \citep{WrightBinney2026}: its median
fractional difference is $62\%$ over Tier~A+B+C ($85\%$ in Tier~A), and
$463$ of the $621$ stars are flagged \texttt{sampled\_poor}.
The catalogue therefore ships orbit-time-averaged actions and a per-star
action-reliability flag alongside the single-evaluation values, and we
treat $J_z$-dependent statements as qualitative.  This caveat is
sharpened in Section~\ref{sec:robustness} alongside the barred-potential
sensitivity test.

\subsection{Matched-Control Sample} \label{sec:controls}

To test whether the slow sample's orbit-derived properties are distinctive
rather than generic to Gaia DR3 6D stars at matched observability, we
constructed a multi-band velocity-stratified control sample from the full
Gaia DR3 6D catalogue.  Four velocity bands were
defined: 25--50, 50--100, 100--200, and 200--260\kms.  Within each band,
controls are kernel-weighted on the slow catalogue's broad
observability marginals in $G$, $\log d$, and $\sin |b|$, with the same
valid-radial-velocity requirement as the slow-star sample.  The effective
control samples contain 1,955, 2,586, 2,788, and 2,749 stars for the
four bands respectively.

The adequacy of this weighting is audited rather than assumed.  For nine
observability covariates---$G$, $\log d$, $|\sin b|$, $\sin l$, $\cos l$,
BP--RP, $\ln\mathrm{RUWE}$, $\log_{10}(\varpi/\sigma_\varpi)$, and
$G_{\mathrm{RVS}}$---we compute the standardized mean difference (SMD)
between each control band and the slow catalogue before and after
weighting (Table~\ref{tab:control_balance};
Fig.~\ref{fig:control_balance}).  The product-of-one-dimensional
observability kernel does not by itself achieve covariate balance:
residual $|\mathrm{SMD}|$ values remain at the $0.6$--$0.8$ level and the
matched marginals are not driven toward zero, because matching each
marginal independently ignores the joint covariate correlations.  We
therefore adopt entropy balancing \citep{Hainmueller2012} as the
principled weighted-inference layer; it solves for the maximum-entropy
control weights that match every covariate mean to
$|\mathrm{SMD}|\lesssim10^{-3}$, with band effective sample sizes of
387, 990, 1{,}438, and 1{,}474.  The across-band ordering of
median pericentre with $\vgrf$ (\S\ref{sec:band_comparison}) is stable
under all four weighting schemes we tested---unweighted, the observability
kernel, propensity-score inverse-probability weighting, and entropy
balancing---so the matched comparison is not an artefact of the
weighting choice.  These reweightings use the existing local control
products rather than a fresh Gaia archive scan or a replacement
control-parent construction.

The 25--50\kms band yields fewer matched controls because the candidate
pool at these velocities is intrinsically small: within the Gaia DR3 6D
parent sample after the basic quality requirements used for control
construction (valid radial velocity and parallax signal-to-noise $> 5$),
there are only 4,763 stars in this range, compared with 39,853 at
50--100\kms and over 1.4 million at 100--200\kms.  Overall, the low-velocity tail is extremely sparse within the observed
Gaia DR3 6D catalogue: 7,622 stars below 50\kms out of roughly 6.8
million stars in that quality-filtered 6D parent sample, or 0.11\%.
All controls were orbit-enriched using the same static Hunter+2024
potential and integration settings as the slow-star sample.

The stratified design tests how orbital properties vary with $\vgrf$,
rather than comparing the slow-star sample to a single control window.
The 25--50\kms{} band is the adjacent kinematic comparison to the
slow-star sample; the 100--200\kms{} band samples a mixture of
thick-disc and inner-halo kinematics; and the 200--260\kms{} band
brackets the circular speed at $\Rsun$ (229\kms) and therefore includes
a substantial disc population.

\subsection{Catalogue Contents and Availability} \label{sec:catalogue}

The accompanying catalogue includes Gaia \texttt{source\_id}, the
observables used for sample construction, adopted Galactocentric
positions and velocities, $\vgrf$, quality flags, and the principal
orbit-derived quantities reported here (eccentricity, $\Rperi$, $\Rapo$,
$\zmax$, and related summary metrics).  Orbit-derived fields are
explicitly labelled as model-dependent outputs under the adopted
potential rather than as direct Gaia observables.  Per-star chemistry is
included where available---Gaia GSP-Phot \MH{} and, for the
APOGEE\,DR17/GALAH\,DR3 cross-match, spectroscopic \FeH{}, an
$\alpha$-abundance proxy, and a chemodynamic-population label, kept
separate from the photometric metallicity---and the orbit catalogue adds
both single-evaluation and orbit-time-averaged actions with a per-star
action-reliability flag.

\subsection{The Primary Catalogue Survives Conservative Quality Cuts}
\label{sec:gold}

The Gold observational-quality subset starts from the Tier~A+B primary catalogue
and then applies only observational quality cuts.  The catalogue
Gold subset contains 226 stars after requiring RUWE $<1.4$, acceptable
RVS quality, parallax signal-to-noise $>10$, and $\sigma_d/d<0.15$.
This gives a compact follow-up list whose definition does not depend on
the orbit model.

Because these cuts are applied to a sample that is itself selected at a
kinematic extreme, it is worth establishing explicitly that they are not
circular.  Two properties make that case, following the argument set out
for age-extremum samples by \citet{Banik2026}.  First, every cut is
defined on an axis distinct from \vgrf{}: astrometric goodness of fit,
image-parameter multiplicity, RVS template quality, parallax
signal-to-noise, and fractional distance precision are all properties of
the observation rather than of the velocity used to select the sample.
Second, and more directly testable, the pass rate of each cut is
essentially flat in \vgrf{} across the full 20{,}829-source candidate
pool (Table~\ref{tab:cut_orthogonality}).  For RUWE, image-parameter
multiplicity, RVS quality, and fractional distance error, the largest
difference in pass rate between any two \vgrf{} bins spanning
$0$--$50\kms$ is $0.009$, $0.011$, $0.019$, and $0.031$ respectively;
a cut that is this nearly blind to \vgrf{} cannot preferentially
manufacture slow stars.  The one partial exception is the parallax
signal-to-noise requirement $\varpi/\sigma_\varpi>10$, which shows a
larger spread ($0.053$) and, when applied alone, removes the largest
share of the catalogue, retaining $88.9\%$ of Tier~A+B+C.  A poorly
measured parallax is one route to a spuriously low speed, so a cut that
is mildly \vgrf{}-dependent in this direction is expected, and we report
it rather than treat it as neutral.  In every case the cuts only ever
remove candidates, so no cut can inflate the catalogue; and the median
\vgrf{} of the survivors moves by at most $0.06\kms$ under any single
cut, and by $-0.05\kms$ when all are applied together.

Unresolved binaries and problematic single-epoch radial velocities can
perturb Gaia RVS velocities on scales comparable to the 25\kms{}
threshold.  We therefore treat RUWE and RVS-quality screens as catalogue
diagnostics rather than proof of single-star centre-of-mass velocities.
In the catalogue products, 14/276 Tier~A+B stars
($5.1^{+1.5}_{-1.2}\%$) and 32/621 Tier~A+B+C stars
($5.2^{+1.0}_{-0.8}\%$) have RUWE $>1.4$ (Jeffreys 68\% intervals throughout);
the Gold subset removes these systems, and the poor-RVS exclusion test
in Section~\ref{sec:robustness} shows that the compact radial-orbit
summary is unchanged after removing flagged RVS solutions.

A direct check against the Gaia DR3 non-single-star catalogue sharpens
this screen.  Cross-matching by \texttt{source\_id} against
\texttt{gaiadr3.nss\_two\_body\_orbit} flags 5 of the 276 Tier~A+B
sources ($1.8\%$) and 13 of the 621 Tier~A+B+C sources ($2.1\%$) as
resolved binaries---predominantly single-lined spectroscopic (SB1)
solutions in the full Tier~A+B+C extension---which are marked in the
released catalogue.  RUWE and the non-single-star tables are most
sensitive to unresolved and to fully resolved companions respectively,
which leaves the marginally resolved regime between them.  We therefore
also apply the Gaia image-parameter cut
\texttt{ipd\_frac\_multi\_peak}$\,\leq 1$, which limits the percentage of
focal-plane transits in which a source is split into multiple peaks
\citep[][adopt a threshold of 2]{Banik2026,Pace2022}.  This retains
$265/276$ ($96.0\%$) of Tier~A+B and $595/621$ ($95.8\%$) of
Tier~A+B+C, and it is largely independent of the RUWE screen, which
removes a comparable but different $5.1\%$ of Tier~A+B.  Applying all
observational cuts jointly, including this one, leaves $220$ Tier~A+B
stars---six fewer than the $226$-star Gold subset defined without
it---and $472$ Tier~A+B+C stars, with the median \vgrf{} moving by
$-0.08$ and $-0.05\kms$ respectively, so the surviving sample is if
anything marginally slower rather than faster.  Beyond these
catalogued binaries, the RV-specific quality indicators bound the residual
contamination (Table~\ref{tab:rv_quality}): the median DR3 RV uncertainty
is $2.4$\kms{} for Tier~A+B, $58\%$ of primary-catalogue sources exceed $2$\kms,
and $6.1\%$ have an RV-constancy $\chi^2$ $p$-value below $0.01$
indicative of RV variability.  Because the median uncertainty is a
non-negligible fraction of the $25$\kms{} threshold, the
$\sigma_{\rm RV}<2$\kms{} cut (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}) and the Gold
subset are the operative defences against RV-driven false positives.

We also queried public APOGEE DR17 and GALAH DR3 radial velocities for
the Tier~A+B+C source IDs as a sparse external consistency audit
(Table~\ref{tab:external_rv}).  The overlap is footprint-selected rather
than complete: 67 Tier~A+B+C stars have APOGEE RVs and 62 have GALAH RVs.
The Gaia-minus-external median offsets are small ($-0.10$\kms{} for
APOGEE and $+0.16$\kms{} for GALAH), with robust scatter of 1.10 and
1.72\kms, respectively.  A minority of overlaps show
$|\Delta RV|>5$\kms{} (6.0\% for APOGEE, 9.7\% for GALAH), so the external
check supports the bulk RV scale but does not replace follow-up
confirmation for individual boundary candidates.

\subsection{Velocity-Threshold Membership Is Probabilistic}
\label{sec:vgrf_membership_results}

The corrected point-estimate catalogue contains 2{,}755 stars below
$\vgrf=25$\kms, but the threshold itself is not equally secure for every
star.  After the population-prior correction this gives 173 stars in Tier~A
($P>0.95$), 276 stars cumulatively in Tier~A+B ($P>0.84$), and
621 stars cumulatively in Tier~A+B+C ($P>0.50$).  The 2{,}134
corrected point-estimate members with $P\leq0.50$ form Tier~D.  The paper therefore
uses Tier~A+B as the primary catalogue and Tier~A+B+C for broader
population-level orbit summaries.  Table~\ref{tab:vgrf_membership}
summarises the tier definitions and counts, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:pvgrf_membership} shows the membership probability as a
function of the corrected point-estimate \vgrf{} across the propagated
pool.

A natural question is whether the low-\vgrf{} population is more numerous
than a smooth continuation of the Gaia DR3 velocity field would imply.
We do not attempt to answer it with a count, and it is worth being
explicit about why.  Any such ratio requires a parent normalisation, and
the matched-control library cannot supply one: it is constructed with
comparable numbers of stars in each velocity band, so its sampling rate
relative to the Gaia 6D catalogue varies by more than two orders of
magnitude between the slowest and fastest bands, and its pooled velocity
distribution is a property of that construction rather than of the
Galaxy.  Normalising a predicted count to the control library while
counting observed slow stars from the complete parent scan compares two
different populations.  More fundamentally, Gaia RVS is magnitude
limited, so this catalogue consists of giants and bright nearby dwarfs
and contains essentially none of the M dwarfs that dominate the stellar
census; no absolute density statement is available to us at any level of
effort.

The latent reconstruction of Section~\ref{sec:latent} answers the
well-posed part of the question directly, and without a normalisation.
Reconstructed from the complete near-threshold pool using each star's own
uncertainties, the latent \vgrf{} distribution rises smoothly and
monotonically through the selection boundary: the curvature of $\ln P$
stays below $0.02$ per bin across $20$--$30\kms$, against a maximum of
$0.08$ elsewhere in the reconstructed range.  The $25\kms$ threshold
leaves no imprint on the recovered distribution.  The low-\vgrf{}
catalogue is therefore a tail selection from a smooth velocity
distribution, not a physically distinct population separated by a
discontinuity, and we make no claim of a number excess.  The
matched-control comparison is retained in
Section~\ref{sec:band_comparison} as a distributional check on orbital
properties, where no parent normalisation is required.

\subsection{The Population Prior Halves the Expected Slow Count}
\label{sec:latent}

The tier probabilities defined above are \emph{forward} scores: for each
star, the fraction of Monte Carlo realisations of its own measurement
errors that fall below $25\kms$.  They carry no information about the
parent population.  That omission matters here, because the candidate
pool rises steeply with \vgrf{}---188 sources below $10\kms$ against
$7{,}635$ between $40$ and $50\kms$---so far more stars are available to
scatter down into the slow window than up out of it.  This is the
classical Eddington bias \citep{Eddington1913}, and the forward scores do
not correct for it.
The injection-recovery calibration of
Section~\ref{sec:injection_recovery} does not close the gap either,
because its mock uses a balanced injected velocity prior by construction.

We therefore reconstruct the latent \vgrf{} distribution of the candidate
pool directly.  Writing $L_{ij}$ for the Monte Carlo likelihood of star
$i$ in latent bin $j$, and $P_j$ for the latent distribution, we iterate
\begin{equation}
w_{ij} = \frac{P_j L_{ij}}{\sum_k P_k L_{ik}}, \qquad
P_j = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i w_{ij},
\label{eq:em}
\end{equation}
to convergence.  This is the iterative rectification of an observed
distribution introduced by \citet{Richardson1972} and \citet{Lucy1974},
equivalently expectation--maximisation
\citep{Dempster1977}; in the Gaia era the same
deconvolution problem is more often approached parametrically, as in the
extreme-deconvolution formalism of \citet{Bovy2011}.  We adopt the
non-parametric binned form because the quantity of interest is the shape
of the distribution near a threshold rather than a mixture description of
it.  Our implementation follows the recent application of this approach by
\citet{Banik2026} to the analogous problem of the oldest-star age
extremum, which is where we encountered it in this context.  The converged
$w_{ij}$ are per-star posteriors with the population prior folded in, so
$\sum_{v_j<25} w_{ij}$ is a revised membership probability
for star $i$.  The likelihood grids come from re-running the
Section~\ref{sec:vgrf_mc} forward model at $1\kms$ resolution, which
reproduces the released per-star probabilities to within Monte Carlo
noise (summed $P$ of $2{,}556.4$ against $2{,}555.9$).  The median
per-star \vgrf{} uncertainty is $8.5\kms$, with a $5$--$95$ percentile
range of $1.7$--$21.1\kms$.

Unregularised \eqref{eq:em} is a maximum-likelihood deconvolution and
develops the bin-to-bin oscillations characteristic of that algorithm at
high iteration count, exactly as \citet{Banik2026} report for their
unsmoothed variant.  We therefore apply a Gaussian smoothing kernel to
$P_j$ after each iteration, the smoothed-EM regularisation of
\citet{Silverman1990}, and scan its width.  The oscillations are confined to the $35$--$50\kms$ region;
below $25\kms$ the reconstructions for kernel widths of $0$, $0.75$,
$1.5$, and $3$ bins are indistinguishable, and the recovered count is
stable from roughly the tenth iteration onwards
(Fig.~\ref{fig:latent_regularised}).

The result is a substantial downward revision, summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:latent_deconvolution}.  The expected number of pool
members with true $\vgrf<25\kms$ falls from the naive sum of forward
probabilities, $2{,}556$, to $1{,}234^{+110}_{-146}$, and the revised
tier counts fall from $289$, $541$, and $1{,}952$ to approximately $173$,
$276$, and $621$.  Two features make this robust.  The reconstruction is
insensitive to the latent-grid ceiling, giving $1{,}186$ to $1{,}198$
expected slow stars for ceilings of $40$ to $80\kms$; and the full spread
across smoothing kernels, $1{,}198$ to $1{,}365$, is comparable to the
bootstrap uncertainty.

Three qualifications belong with this number.  First, the correction is
conservative: the candidate pool was screened at a preliminary
$\vgrf<50\kms$ and is complete only below that, so latent bins above the
screen are under-populated relative to the real Galaxy and down-scatter
from genuinely fast stars is under-represented.  The true contamination
can only be larger than reported here.  Second, we treat the Monte Carlo
posterior of each star as its likelihood, which is exact under a flat
prior in \vgrf{} and approximate given the \citet{BailerJones2021}
distance prior; the same approximation is made by \citet{Banik2026}.
Third, and most importantly for the released products, the correction is
close to monotonic in the forward score, so the \emph{ranking} of the
catalogue is preserved even though its absolute calibration shifts.  No
star enters a population-prior tier that was not already in the
corresponding forward tier, and the two scores have rank correlations of
$\rho_{\rm S}=0.997$ and $\tau_{\rm K}=0.963$.  Retiering therefore moves
the thresholds without reordering the catalogue, which is why we adopt
the population-prior scores as the tier definition
(Section~\ref{sec:vgrf_membership_results}) rather than carrying the
correction only as a caveat: the forward thresholds of $0.95$, $0.84$,
and $0.50$ correspond to population-prior thresholds of $0.987$, $0.957$,
and $0.813$, so the released tiers were simply cut too loosely.  Both
scores ship with the catalogue, so either definition can be reproduced.
Because the tiers are nested, every orbit product quoted below was
already computed for every retained star; the retiering re-aggregates
those products over a subset and integrates no new orbits.

\subsection{Chemistry Does Not Define the Sample} \label{sec:chemistry}

Metallicity information is used here only as a diagnostic of chemical
heterogeneity and estimator bias, not for population assignment.  The
metallicity spread in the matched subset is broad enough that the
catalogue should not be treated as a single chemical population.

These official Gaia DR3 GSP-Phot metallicities are low-resolution photometric
estimates with documented systematics
\citep{Andrae2023, RecioBlanco2023}: in particular, GSP-Phot tends to
overestimate metallicity for cool, metal-poor giants by 0.2--0.5\,dex,
which biases the present sample toward solar values.  In the catalogue, GSP-Phot metallicities are finite for 1{,}279 Tier~A+B+C stars
and have a median $\MH=-0.24$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:metallicity}).  The spectroscopic alpha-subset discussed
below is more metal-poor, with median $\FeH=-0.81$.  This offset is not
interpreted as a population difference: it reflects both the known
GSP-Phot metallicity systematics and the strongly non-random targeting of
the APOGEE/GALAH overlap.  The \citet{Zhang2023} XP catalogue remains the
preferred larger photometric metallicity comparison for a future broader
cross-match, but the present paper uses only the synchronized GSP-Phot
and spectroscopic products as release materials.

\subsection{Chemical Properties from Spectroscopic Cross-Matching}
\label{sec:spectroscopy}

We cross-matched the full 20{,}829-source candidate pool against
APOGEE DR17 \citep{Abdurrauf2022} and GALAH DR3 \citep{Buder2021} using
exact Gaia source identifiers rather than positional matching.  The query
returns 604 APOGEE matches and 489 GALAH matches across the candidate
pool.  Spectroscopic abundances are rare here: 66 stars have usable
APOGEE $\alpha$-element abundances and 59 have usable GALAH abundances,
and after survey-priority deduplication 117 stars carry the joint
\FeH{}~$+$~$\alpha$ combination required for the literature-region
comparisons.  Restricting that set to the stars above the adopted velocity
threshold would leave only 48.

We do not impose that restriction.  The velocity threshold is not a
chemical criterion, and a star does not stop being chemically informative
because its rest-frame speed is more likely $30\kms$ than $22\kms$.  We
therefore report the chemistry on the full 117-star $\alpha$-classified
sample and quantify the membership difference per region rather than
cutting on it (Table~\ref{tab:population_decomp}): alongside the raw count
we give $\Sigma P_{\rm pop}$, the summed population-prior probability,
which is the effective number of genuinely slow stars each region
contributes, and the count surviving the $P>0.5$ threshold.  The summed
probability over the whole $\alpha$-classified sample is $58.5$.

The three treatments give consistent proportions---Splash $43.6\%$,
$44.6\%$, $45.8\%$; Gaia-Sausage/Enceladus $19.7\%$, $17.9\%$, $18.8\%$;
Aurora $18.8\%$, $19.5\%$, $20.8\%$ by raw count, by summed probability,
and after thresholding respectively---so the mixed chemical character of
the slow sample does not depend on where the velocity threshold is
placed.  That robustness, rather than any individual fraction, is the
result we draw from this subset.  Per-star population-prior probabilities
ship with the catalogue, so these regions can be recut at any threshold.
This $\alpha$-classified subset is dominated by luminous giants
in the APOGEE and GALAH footprints; it is therefore a selective but
useful chemical window, not a chemically complete census.  The APOGEE
$[\mathrm{Mg/Fe}]$ and GALAH $[\alpha/\mathrm{Fe}]$ scales are not
homogenised onto a single abundance system in this release, so the pooled
classification is used only as a literature-region context flag.  Survey
counts are reported separately, and no whole-catalogue population fraction
is inferred from the mixed-survey subset.

Within the 117-star $\alpha$-classified sample, 51 stars fall in a
Splash-like region, 23 in a Gaia-Sausage/Enceladus-like region, 22 in an
Aurora-like region, 2 in a disc-like contaminant region, and 19 are not
assigned to these literature regions.  Weighting each star by its
population-prior probability gives $26.1$, $10.5$, $11.4$, $1.4$, and
$9.0$ respectively; thresholding at $P>0.5$ gives 22, 9, 10, 1, and 6.
These counts are reported only within the $\alpha$-classified sample.
They are not used as whole-catalogue population fractions, because the
remaining Tier~A+B+C stars lack the high-resolution abundance
information required for that assignment in the current release products.

The broader metallicity context is still informative.  Gaia GSP-Phot
metallicities are available for many candidates, but the comparison in
Section~\ref{sec:chemistry} shows that GSP-Phot is biased toward solar
metallicity for the cool, metal-poor giants that dominate this sample.
We therefore use GSP-Phot only as a low-resolution heterogeneity check
and rely on spectroscopic abundances for subset-qualified population
language.  Photometric metallicity alone does not supply the
alpha-element information needed for the Splash/GSE/Aurora comparison
cuts.

\subsection{Sky Distribution, Observer Geometry, and Selection}
\label{sec:sky}

The sample is not isotropically distributed on the sky
(Fig.~\ref{fig:sky}).  In Galactic
coordinates, $39.7\pm 1.1\%$ of the Tier~A+B+C stars lie toward the
Galactic-centre direction ($l \in [330\degr, 30\degr]$), while only
$6.2^{+0.6}_{-0.5}\%$ lie toward the anti-centre direction
($l \in [150\degr, 210\degr]$).  Latitude coverage is broad, from about
$-85\degr$ to $+87\degr$, with a slight southern bias in the median
latitude.

This concentration is a prominent descriptive feature of the
observed catalogue.  It likely reflects some combination of heliocentric
observing geometry, the Gaia radial-velocity selection function,
extinction and crowding effects, and the radial character of the
underlying trajectories.  The velocity-stratified controls are also
centre-weighted, indicating that the sky distribution is partly a generic
property of observed Gaia 6D stars at these distances.

The \citet{CastroGinard2023} Gaia DR3 RVS selection function (extending
the EDR3 RVS sample-completeness framework of \citealt{EverallBoubert2022})
is therefore used as a limitation and contextual observability diagnostic
for the observed catalogue rather than as a precise deprojection of the
underlying Milky Way population.  We evaluate the catalogue with
the GaiaUnlimited DR3-RVS implementation and release the per-star
selection-function table, but inverse-selection weighted counts are not
used as primary population counts.

\subsection{In the Adopted Model, the Sample Is Strongly Radially
  Biased} \label{sec:eccentricity}

Within the adopted static Hunter+2024 potential, the orbit-derived
picture is strongly radial.  For the Tier~A+B+C catalogue
($N=621$), the median of the per-star posterior-median
eccentricities is 0.965.  All 621 stars have $e>0.8$, $72.6\pm 1.8\%$
have a posterior-median $e>0.95$, and $1.9^{+0.6}_{-0.5}\%$ have a
posterior-median $e>0.99$ from the 5{,}000-realisation Monte Carlo orbit
product (Table~\ref{tab:orbit_summary}; Jeffreys 68\% intervals).

The corresponding fractions obtained from a single deterministic
point-estimate orbit integration in the same potential
(Table~\ref{tab:threshold}) are higher: $66.7\pm 1.1\%$ of Tier~A+B+C
stars have $e>0.95$ under point-estimate integration.  The difference
between the Monte Carlo posterior-median fraction (48.2\%) and the
point-estimate fraction (66.7\%) is the expected consequence of
convolving the measurement-error distribution into a quantity that is
bounded above by unity: the per-star posterior medians regress slightly
toward lower $e$ relative to the noise-free point estimate.  Both
numbers describe the same catalogue; we report the posterior-median
fraction as the primary value because it propagates the observational
uncertainties explicitly.

 The same Monte Carlo orbit product gives a Tier~A+B+C median cylindrical
 $\Rperi$ of 154\,pc and a median cylindrical $\Rapo$ of 7.35\kpc.  The maximum posterior
median $\Rapo$ in the present Tier~A+B+C integrations is 17.86\kpc.
These values confirm that the low-speed catalogue is not
dominated by ordinary near-circular disc kinematics.  This compact,
radial mapping is also physically expected for a catalogue selected at
very low total Galactic rest-frame speed: in the adopted Galactocentric
frame, such stars carry little angular momentum at the observed epoch.
The qualitative compact-pericentre, high-radiality tendency is therefore
 robust across the tested potentials; the precise per-star cylindrical pericentres are
not, because the real inner Galaxy is barred and non-axisymmetric.

The same integrations settle what the orbits do at closest approach, which
a small pericentre alone does not determine.  The azimuth swept between
successive apocentres is $2\pi\,\Omega_\phi/\Omega_R$: it is $360\degr$
for a closed Kepler ellipse, in which the star returns along its inbound
track, and $180\degr$ for a harmonic, constant-density core, in which
successive apocentres lie on opposite sides of the centre.  For the
Tier~A+B+C catalogue the median is $195\degr$, with a $16$th--$84$th
percentile range of $155\degr$--$202\degr$, and no member exceeds
$300\degr$; the median is the same to within $3\degr$ in each individual
tier.  These are therefore transiting rather than reversing orbits: the
star crosses the inner Galaxy and emerges on the far side, and its
pericentre is a rapid passage rather than a turning point at which the
motion is undone.  The proximity of the median to the harmonic value is
consistent with orbits that spend their perigalactic phase inside the
region where the rotation curve is still rising.  The frequencies are
St\"ackel-fudge outputs and inherit the accuracy caveats of
Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}, so we read the distribution as a whole
rather than individual per-star values; the conclusion rests on its
distance from $360\degr$, which is far larger than the fudge error.

These quantities should be read as outputs of the adopted model rather
than as direct Gaia observables.  They are useful because they provide a
 coherent dynamical interpretation of the slow state.  Cylindrical pericentre
estimates below roughly 200\pc should be treated as indicative, because
the real inner Galaxy is barred and non-axisymmetric on those scales; the
barred Hunter/Sormani variant in Section~\ref{sec:robustness} brackets
this sensitivity.  Satellite-orbit studies likewise find that
pericentres are often more stable than orbit type or apocentre under
large-scale potential changes \citep{Li2021,Pace2022}, but those
satellite-scale results are not used here as a quantitative error model
for sub-kpc inner-Galaxy reaches.  They instead motivate the emphasis on
 population distributions, matched-control trends, and threshold counts
 rather than exact per-star cylindrical turning points or three-dimensional
 closest approaches.

Crucially, the orbital \emph{shape} is robust to the choice of
axisymmetric potential.  Re-integrating the catalogue in the
independently calibrated, Gaia-DR3-external \citet{McMillan2017} potential
gives a near-identical point-estimate summary---median eccentricity
0.968, median $\Rperi=138$\,pc, median $\Rapo=8.24$\kpc, all 621
stars with $e>0.8$,
and three static long-apocentre inner-reach candidates---so the radial, compact-pericentre character is
not an artefact of the non-Gaia-DR3-calibrated Hunter+2024 model
 (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}).  What \emph{is} potential-sensitive is
 the precise cylindrical spin-axis reach and the barred long-apocentre inner-reach count,
which vary by a factor of several between the static and barred families
(\S\ref{sec:reach}; Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}).  We therefore treat
 the high-eccentricity, small-$\Rperi$ summary as robust and the exact
inner-Galaxy reach as model-dependent.

\subsection{Matched-Control Comparison} \label{sec:band_comparison}

A velocity-stratified, observability-matched control comparison provides
the clearest test of whether the slow-star compact-pericentre and
high-eccentricity orbit summaries merely reflect Gaia stars at similar
distances, or whether they vary systematically with Galactic rest-frame
speed.  The
controls are weighted toward the slow catalogue's broad observability
marginals, and the covariate balance of that weighting is audited in
Section~\ref{sec:controls}.  In the catalogue, the slow-sample
point-estimate median $\Rperi$ is 115.5\,pc.  The control-band median
pericentres rise monotonically with $\vgrf$ under every weighting scheme;
the exact values are method-dependent but the ordering and the slow
sample's compact-pericentre endpoint are not.  Under the
covariate-balanced entropy weights (Table~\ref{tab:control_balance}) the
four control medians are 206\,pc, 592\,pc, 2.68\kpc, and 3.97\kpc; under
the observability kernel they are 132.6\,pc, 408\,pc, 1.36\kpc, and
2.25\kpc; and unweighted they are 143\,pc, 460\,pc, 1.76\kpc, and
2.82\kpc.  In every case the slow catalogue sits at the compact-pericentre
extreme of the matched controls.  We read this matched comparison as a null
check that the slow sample's compact pericentres are not generic to Gaia 6D
stars at similar observability, not as a calibrated speed--pericentre
relation: the controls are a deliberately broad, selection-function-limited
comparison over four coarse velocity bands, and the across-band gradient
runs in the direction expected from the kinematics of the cut.

The same comparison provides a direct check on bar-resonance
accumulation.  The slow sample is not uniquely enhanced at the OLR:
its weighted OLR fraction is 1.84\%, lower than the 100--200 and
200--260\kms{} controls and only modestly higher than the adjacent
low-velocity controls.  Barred integrations therefore remain important
for individual inner-Galaxy orbits and for the long-apocentre inner-reach sensitivity test,
but the matched-control comparison does not support OLR accumulation as
the primary explanation for the catalogue.  Figure~\ref{fig:resonances}
in Section~\ref{sec:elz} places the catalogue in $L_z$--$J_R$ action
space alongside the inner Lindblad, corotation, and outer Lindblad
loci for the full $\Omega_p$ grid; the Tier~A+B+C distribution
concentrates near $L_z\approx0$ rather than at any single
pattern-speed-resonance pile-up.

The full eccentricity CDFs reinforce the same point.  An unweighted two-sample
Anderson--Darling comparison \citep{ScholzStephens1987} between the
Tier~A+B+C static-potential eccentricities and the adjacent 25--50\kms{}
matched-control CDF rejects the hypothesis of a common eccentricity
distribution at asymptotic $p<0.001$.  The same test against an
eccentricity realisation drawn from a single trivariate-Gaussian velocity
ellipsoid fitted to the control library likewise rejects at asymptotic
$p<0.001$ (the mock realisation and the script that regenerates it
from the released control library are included in the data bundle).  Thus the near-unity eccentricity concentration is
not reproduced by either the adjacent observed control band or that
smooth velocity ellipsoid.  Both are statements about the \emph{shape} of
the eccentricity distribution and require no parent normalisation, which
is why they survive the considerations of
Section~\ref{sec:vgrf_membership_results}.  The monotonic eccentricity
decline across the four control bands is likewise recovered under the
covariate-balanced control weights.  Fig.~\ref{fig:e_vs_vgrf} shows the
weighted per-band median eccentricities (with the full per-band
distributions in Fig.~\ref{fig:e_by_band}), and
Fig.~\ref{fig:rperi_rapo_bands} the corresponding per-band pericentre
medians.

\subsection{Exploratory Inner-Galaxy Reach and Selection Limits} \label{sec:reach}
\label{sec:long_reach}

Although the catalogue shares the defining low-\vgrf{} state, the stars
span a range of model-derived orbital scales.  The Tier~A+B+C catalogue
 has a median static-potential cylindrical pericentre of 153\,pc in the orbit
Monte Carlo posterior medians, and all 621 posterior-median
 cylindrical pericentres remain inside 1\,kpc.  In the dedicated point-estimate
 cylindrical-reach diagnostic, $\Rperi$ is the cylindrical in-plane
pericentre, $\min\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, i.e. closest approach to the Galactic
spin axis rather than to Sgr~A* as a point.  With this definition,
241 stars ($38.8\%$) have static $\Rperi<100$\,pc and all 621 have static
$\Rperi<1$\kpc.  This fact is visible in the
 right panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:face_on}: every star's model cylindrical
 pericentre falls within the inner kpc in $R$, despite the catalogue
being observed across the Gaia DR3 heliocentric survey volume shown in
the left panel.  In the barred
sensitivity integration the corresponding cylindrical point-estimate
count is 1{,}635 stars with $\Rperi<100$\,pc; those barred quantities
are used as sensitivity diagnostics rather than as the default catalogue
 values.  The conservative cylindrical inner-reach statement is therefore stronger
than the exact 100-pc scale: in the adopted static point-estimate model,
the full catalogue lies well inside common bar/bulge apertures
 ($R<3.5$--$5$\kpc), while the parsec-scale cylindrical medians and counts remain
model diagnostics.

\needspace{8\baselineskip}

We additionally propagated dedicated 5{,}000-realisation Monte Carlo
closest approaches to the Galactic centre for the closest static and
barred seeds: no candidate has $P(r_{\rm sph,min}<10\,{\rm pc})>0.5$ and
six have $P(r_{\rm sph,min}<100\,{\rm pc})>0.5$, so we describe these as
candidate inner-Galaxy approachers rather than a confirmed
Sgr~A*-passing population. The closest-approach probabilities and
cumulative curves are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:sgrA}
(Table~\ref{tab:sgrA_candidates}, Fig.~\ref{fig:sgrA_cdfs}).

The closest-approach calculation follows the same general philosophy as
Gaia DR3 stellar-encounter work, where orbit integration and uncertainty
propagation are used to identify robust close-passage candidates rather
than to treat nominal closest approaches as definitive
\citep{BailerJones2022}.

As an external source-id check, none of the 276 Tier~A+B stars overlaps
the 61 Gaia DR3 close-encounter entries in \citet{BailerJones2022}.

We also checked the closest published low-azimuthal-velocity comparison
sample, the 69 slow- and retrograde APOGEE/Gaia stars of
\citet{Filion2025}.  All 69 resolve to unique Gaia DR3 sources and pass
the present Gaia-RVS parent-source contract, but none enters
Tier~A+B+C under the present low-\vgrf{} probability tiering.  Three
enter the 20{,}829-source Monte Carlo candidate pool and remain Tier~X.
The detailed comparison is reported in Appendix~\ref{app:filion}
(Table~\ref{tab:filion2025_comparison}); it confirms that the difference
is primarily one of selection definition---low $V_\phi$ is not the same
as low total \vgrf{}---rather than an exact source-id overlap.

The radial-reach tail is also potential-sensitive.  Three Tier~A+B+C
stars (Poisson 68\% band $1.4$--$5.9$) satisfy the long-apocentre
inner-reach criterion
($\Rperi<2$\kpc{} and $\Rapo>15$\kpc) in the static Hunter+2024
integration.  The barred default integration produces 21 such candidates
(68\% Poisson band $16.5$--$26.7$); across the pattern-speed grid
explored in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}, the count ranges from 7 to 31.
Because the absolute count varies by a factor of several between the
static and default-barred integrations and remains $\Omega_p$-sensitive
within the barred family, we treat these stars as bar-sensitive
orbital-geometry candidates whose count is intrinsically
potential-dependent, rather than as a robust population count or a
merger-debris census.

\subsection{Energy and Angular-Momentum Distribution}
\label{sec:elz}

A standard Gaia-era diagnostic is the orbital energy versus
$z$-component of angular momentum ($E$--$L_z$); the same diagnostic
underlies the substructure surveys of \citet{Naidu2020} and the
proto-Galaxy mapping of \citet{Rix2022}.  In the static Hunter+2024
potential, the Tier~A+B+C catalogue occupies low angular momentum and
strongly bound inner-Galaxy phase space---the near-zero-$L_z$ regime
probed by earlier solar-neighbourhood studies \citep{Hunt2016}.  Hunt
et al.\ reported a dearth of disc stars near zero angular momentum,
consistent with strong nuclear scattering of low-$L_z$ orbits; that
result is not a contradiction of the present sample because our selection
is not a disc-only solar-neighbourhood distribution, but it is a useful
caution that compact-pericentre low-$L_z$ orbits should not be read as
long-lived regular disc orbits.  This supports the same
catalogue-first interpretation used throughout the paper: the sample
overlaps familiar Gaia-era accretion and in-situ regions, but the
present work does not assign progenitor membership from $E$--$L_z$ alone.
The per-star catalogue reports $E$, $L_z$, and St\"ackel-fudge actions
for follow-up clustering work.

concentrate near $L_z\approx0$ at large $J_R$, away from any single
corotation or outer-Lindblad pile-up, consistent with the matched-control

\subsection{Pipeline Injection-Recovery}
\label{sec:injection_recovery}

To measure the direct pipeline efficiency, we injected controlled
kinematic realisations into GeDR3mock sightlines, photometry, stellar
parameters, and Gaia-like uncertainties \citep{Rybizki2020}.  The mock
contains 10{,}000 stars in each true-\vgrf{} interval
$0$--$25$, $25$--$50$, $50$--$100$, and $100$--$200$\kms, after the
initial $G<17$, $G_{\rm RVS}<14$, and RVS-template-temperature cuts.
Each injected star is passed through the same Lindegren zero-point,
distance-posterior, $\varpi_{\rm corr}/\sigma_\varpi>5$, and Gaia DR3
RVS-availability stages used by the catalogue, plus a fixed
500-realisation velocity-threshold Monte Carlo diagnostic pass.  The
released catalogue itself uses the adaptive 500/5{,}000/10{,}000
schedule described above.  For consistency with the v2
selection-function audit, the RVS-availability draw preserves the v1
GaiaUnlimited convention $g=G_{\rm RVS}$ and $c=BP{-}RP$.
Table~\ref{tab:recovery_summary} and Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery_heatmap}
show that 6{,}038 of 10{,}000 true $0$--$25$\kms{} injections are
recovered as Tier~A+B+C, while only 313 of 10{,}000 stars in the
adjacent $25$--$50$\kms{} decade leak into the slow catalogue and the
higher-velocity leakage is negligible.  Conditional on entering the
Monte Carlo after the RVS and parallax-SNR filters, the slow-bin
recovery is $6{,}038/6{,}944=87.0\%$; end-to-end, including Gaia/RVS
observability, it is $60.4\%$.  Combined with the latent-distribution
reconstruction of Section~\ref{sec:latent} and the selection-function
prior-dominance audit in Section~\ref{sec:interpretation}, the
injection-recovery test forms the catalogue's completeness triad: the
latent \vgrf{} distribution passes smoothly through the selection
boundary, so the catalogue is a tail selection rather than a distinct
population; its effective-volume scaling is limited by sparse
selection-function cells; and the pipeline recovers a quantified fraction
of true synthetic slow stars.

\section{Interpretation and Limits} \label{sec:interpretation}
% =============================================================================

Empirically, Gaia DR3 contains a
tiered catalogue of stars observed at very low Galactic rest-frame speed:
276 stars at Tier~A+B confidence and 621 stars at Tier~A+B+C confidence.
The exact $\vgrf=25$\kms{} boundary is less secure for individual
near-threshold sources, so the catalogue is explicitly probabilistic
rather than a single hard-cut list.

A velocity-stratified control comparison across four bands shows that the
slow sample is more eccentric and compact-pericentre than matched controls
at higher Galactic rest-frame speed, with the across-band gradient running
in the expected kinematic direction.  We read this as a null check that the
slow sample's compact-pericentre character is not generic to Gaia DR3 6D
stars at matched observability, not as a calibrated speed--pericentre
relation: the broad, selection-function-limited control over four coarse
bands is not adequate to establish such a law, and the gradient's direction
is in any case largely set by the kinematics of the cut.

The dwell-time analysis (Section~\ref{sec:dwell}) and the
matched-control gradient (Section~\ref{sec:band_comparison}) together
make a single physical statement.  A kinematic cut at very low $\vgrf$
selects stars on inner-reaching, radially biased orbits during a
low-speed phase.  This is related to the same orbital-turning-point
mechanism discussed in the accretion-debris literature, but the present
catalogue does not identify the selected stars with GS/E or any other
progenitor.  We emphasise that this strong radial bias is in part a
 kinematic corollary of the selection itself.  In the standard spherical
 limit, spherical-radius pericentre and apocentre are the two turning-point roots of
the radial energy equation, and eccentricity is set by those roots for
given energy and angular momentum \citep{BinneyTremaine2008,
HattoriYoshii2010,HattoriYoshii2011}.  At fixed observed position, a
star with $\vgrf \to 0$ has little kinetic energy, so a bound orbit is
being observed at, or very near, an outer turning point.  Because the same
limit also drives $|\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{v}|$ toward zero, there is
little centrifugal support to keep the next half-orbit near that same
Galactocentric radius; in an ordinary centrally concentrated Galactic
potential, the trajectory must move back toward smaller radii until it
reaches its inner turning point.  This generalizes the observability
geometry noted by \citet{Hunt2024} in the context of disc radial phase
spirals, where a fixed local volume preferentially catches low-$L_z$
 stars close to apocentre.  High eccentricity and compact model
 cylindrical pericentres are therefore largely expected from the cut, wherever within
the catalogue's observed Galactocentric range the slow state is measured.
 The model-dependent questions are the exact parsec-scale cylindrical
 spin-axis reach and, more stringently, the three-dimensional Galactic-centre
 closest approach, not the direction of the radiality inference.  The radial bias itself is
thus largely a property of the selection; what the catalogue contributes is
the slow sample, its spatial and orbital structure, and its count relative
to smooth velocity-field continuations of the controls
(\S\ref{sec:vgrf_membership_results}).

The point-estimate orbit integration provides the current
characterisation for the main dynamical claim.  All 621 Tier~A+B+C
stars have $e>0.8$ in the default static potential, and $74.9\pm 1.7\%$
have $e>0.95$ under point-estimate integration (cf.\ the Monte Carlo
posterior-median fraction in Section~\ref{sec:eccentricity}).  The strongly radial
character of the sample is therefore clear in the adopted model, while
posterior orbit uncertainties and potential-variant tests remain
sensitivity products rather than catalogue-definition steps.

It is worth stating explicitly for how long these integrations carry
information about a real star, because an orbit integrated for several
gigayears increasingly describes the adopted potential rather than the
object.  The predictive content is front-loaded.  A star's present
position and velocity are measured, so the direction of its next
excursion is an observable rather than a model output: a star observed at
$\vgrf<25\kms$ near an outer turning point is falling inward next,
whichever centrally concentrated potential is assumed, and the radial
phase reported in Table~\ref{tab:radial_phase} uses the adopted
Galactocentric frame but no orbit integration and no potential at all.
What the potential supplies is the
depth and timing of that first approach.  Beyond it the information
decays quickly.  The median radial period of the catalogue is $91$\,Myr
(16th--84th percentile $80$--$109$\,Myr), and in the default barred
potential Agama's normalised finite-time Lyapunov indicator has a median
of $0.45$ over the sampled chaos subset (96 stars;
Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}), so neighbouring trajectories separate by
a factor $e$ in roughly two radial periods---of order $200$\,Myr, or
about two pericentric passages.  We therefore treat the first inward
excursion as the interval over which a per-star trajectory is
meaningfully constrained, and read everything beyond it, including the
orbital history implied by integrating backwards, as a statistical
property of the ensemble in the adopted potential rather than as the
past of any individual star.  This is a limitation of the data as much as
of the method: a genuinely predictive long-baseline integration would
require a time-dependent Milky Way model, including the bar's history and
the response of the disc, which present observations do not constrain.

A present-day clustering null test (a DBSCAN sensitivity grid over
$\varepsilon=\{5,10,15,25,50\}$\,pc and
\texttt{min\_samples}$=\{3,4,5,7,10\}$;
\citealt{Ester1996,Schubert2017}) returns zero compact groups for both
Tier~A+B and Tier~A+B+C, so the catalogue is not explained by present-day
bound clusters or compact associations; this does not rule out common
accretion-related origins or action-space substructure, which need not
remain compact in present-day position. The grid and nearest-pair
statistics are in Appendix~\ref{app:dbscan} (Table~\ref{tab:cluster_search}).
This present-day compactness check is deliberately narrower than the
standard integral-of-motion and action-space clustering programme for
identifying accreted debris
\citep{HelmiDeZeeuw2000,Myeong2018Streams,Myeong2018Shards,
Koppelman2019,Lovdal2022,RuizLara2022,Dodd2023}.  Density-based
searches, including DBSCAN-like searches in integral-of-motion space
\citep{Borsato2020}, ask whether stars group into common-origin debris
in approximately conserved coordinates.  Our null test instead asks
whether the low-\vgrf{} catalogue is simply one or more compact
present-day spatial aggregates; it is not a progenitor-assignment or
full action-space substructure analysis.

The spectroscopic cross-match sharpens the physical picture while
stopping short of an origin claim.  The alpha-classified subset contains
only 117 Tier~A+B+C stars, so population-region fractions are quoted only
inside that subset.  The sample overlaps phase-space regions often
discussed in the Gaia-era accretion literature \citep{Belokurov2018,
Helmi2018,Belokurov2023}, and the high-eccentricity chemistry context
has been studied with much larger spectroscopic samples
\citep{Lee2023}.  This paper is not a membership or
merger-decomposition analysis.  Because the sample is selected on
instantaneous state, not on origin, chemistry, action space, or a fully
forward-modelled selection function, we do not attempt progenitor
assignment here.

As a selection-function scale diagnostic, we compute
$V_{\rm eff}=\sum_i 1/p_{{\rm sel},i}$ over stars with valid
GaiaUnlimited cells.  For Tier~A+B, excluding cells that sit exactly at
the GaiaUnlimited prior mean gives $V_{\rm eff}=423$; including those
cells at the prior gives $V_{\rm eff}=885$, a factor of 2.09 larger.  For
Tier~A+B+C the corresponding values are $V_{\rm eff}=1{,}512$ and
3{,}406, a factor of 2.25.  The difference is therefore best read as a
selection-function fallback systematic, not as a volume-complete
population normalization.

The prior-dominated (low-parent-count, $n<10$) fraction can be quantified directly from the underlying
GaiaUnlimited $(G,\,BP{-}RP,\,\mathrm{HEALPix})$ grid.  For Tier~A+B,
$83.2\%$ of valid stars sit in cells with parent count $n<10$ and
contribute $88.7\%$ of $\sum 1/S_{\rm RVS}$
(Table~\ref{tab:selection_function}; Fig.~\ref{fig:sf_map});
restricting to the inner-Galaxy quadrant $l\in[330\degr,30\degr]$
where the catalogue concentrates lowers this to $69.7\%$ of stars and
$76.8\%$ of $\sum 1/S_{\rm RVS}$ -- still majority prior-dominated.
The choice of selection-function estimator is a smaller effect.
Combining the \citet{CantatGaudin2023} DR3 parent-completeness map with
the \citet{CastroGinard2023} RVS subsample SF leaves
$\sum 1/S_{\rm RVS}$ for Tier~A+B unchanged at $885$ to within $0.1\%$
because the DR3 parent completeness saturates at the catalogue's
$G_{RVS}\!<\!14$ magnitude range.  Substituting the older EDR3 RVS SF
of \citet{EverallBoubert2022} yields a nominally $\sim\!18\times$
larger $\sum 1/S$, but that estimator targets a smaller, brighter
RVS subsample than DR3 RVS and is not a like-for-like replacement.
We therefore retain the prior-dominance audit as the operative
SF systematic for the catalogue.

The principal open questions are interpretive rather than catalogue-level.
Spectroscopic coverage is incomplete.
 Very small inferred cylindrical pericentres and long-apocentre inner-reach counts are model-sensitive.
The sample is not volume-complete, and local halo samples cannot in
general be extrapolated to the global halo without strong modelling
assumptions \citep{Sharpe2024}.  These limitations set the boundary of
the present paper's claims.

\section{Conclusions} \label{sec:conclusions}
% =============================================================================

We present a tiered, probabilistic Gaia DR3 catalogue of stars with very
low Galactic rest-frame speeds: a primary Tier~A+B catalogue of 276 stars
($P(\vgrf<25\kms)>0.84$), a high-confidence Tier~A core of 173 ($P>0.95$),
and a high-completeness Tier~A+B+C extension of 621 ($P>0.50$).  Membership
probabilities are corrected for the population prior; the uncorrected
forward scores select 289, 541, and 1{,}952 stars at the same thresholds
and are also released.  Each
source carries a Monte Carlo membership probability, so the catalogue is a
probability-scored sample rather than a hard-threshold list.

 In the axisymmetrized Hunter+2024 potential the sample is strongly radial
 with compact cylindrical pericentres (Monte Carlo posterior-median eccentricity 0.949,
$\Rperi=154$\,pc, $\Rapo=7.35$\kpc).  This radial character is largely a
corollary of the selection---a near-zero instantaneous speed picks out
stars near an orbital turning point---and is robust across the halo-mass,
barred, and second-potential (McMillan+2017, Portail+2017) tests of
Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}.  The matched-control comparison is a null
check, not a calibrated relation: against observability-matched samples at
higher $\vgrf$ the slow catalogue is distinctly more eccentric and
 compact in cylindrical pericentre, and the across-band gradient runs in the expected
kinematic sense, but the controls are a deliberately broad,
selection-function-limited comparison over four coarse velocity bands.  We
therefore conclude that the slow state is not a generic property of matched
6D stars, and we do not claim a quantitative speed--eccentricity
law---that would require a substantially larger, selection-corrected
control sample.

 The exact cylindrical inner-Galaxy reach is model-dependent and we treat it as such.
The long-apocentre inner-reach count rises from three in the static
potential to 7--31 across the barred pattern-speed grid
($\Omega_p=24$, 28, 33, 37.5, $41\kms\kpc^{-1}$), with an independent
Portail+2017 M2M bar giving a consistent 15 (Table~\ref{tab:reach}); the
parsec-scale Sgr~A* approach is weaker still---six soft candidates defined
only by Monte Carlo posterior probability, none secure below 10\,pc
(Appendix~\ref{app:sgrA}).  St\"ackel-fudge actions are reliable in $J_R$
and $L_z$ but not $J_z$ for these plunging orbits, so the catalogue ships
orbit-time-averaged actions and a per-star reliability flag alongside the
single-evaluation values.

The orbit products are also bounded in time, and we would rather state
this than leave it implied.  What the data fix is the next excursion:
a star observed at $\vgrf<25\kms$ is near an outer turning point and is
falling inward next, and that follows from its measured velocity rather
than from any potential.  What the model supplies is how deep and how
soon.  With a median radial period of $91$\,Myr and a barred finite-time
Lyapunov indicator implying $e$-folding trajectory separation in roughly
two radial periods, per-star integrations stop being predictive after of
order $200$\,Myr---about two pericentric passages.  Statements beyond
that horizon, in either time direction, should be read as ensemble
properties in the adopted potential and not as the history or future of
an individual star.  Closing that gap needs a time-dependent Galaxy
model, including the bar's history and the disc's response, which the
present data do not constrain.

Completeness, not membership, is the dominant limitation: most of the
catalogue occupies sparse, prior-dominated Gaia~RVS selection-function
cells, so inverse-selection sums are reported only as observability context
and the catalogue is not volume-complete.  Bar-induced chaos is present but
bounded, and the St\"ackel-fudge $J_z$ caveat above means the orbital-shape
statistics are descriptors rather than selection-defining.  Chemistry is
subset-qualified---only 117 Tier~A+B+C stars carry the joint
\FeH{}$+\alpha$ abundances needed for literature-region comparison---and we
make no whole-catalogue population assignment.  A present-day DBSCAN search
finds no bound clusters, consistent with a kinematically rather than
spatially defined sample.

The natural next step is Gaia DR4: the same probabilistic low-\vgrf{}
selection on a longer baseline will test the stability of the present
sample, retire the prior-dominated colour--magnitude cells that set the
selection-function systematic, and enlarge the accessible low-speed
catalogue.

\section{Robustness and Sensitivity Battery} \label{app:sensitivity}

The principal dynamical claims are tested here against quality filtering,
threshold variation, measurement uncertainty, distance estimator, Solar
frame, halo mass, and barred-potential sensitivity.

\paragraph{Gold-subset dynamical robustness in the adopted model.}
The Gold dynamical subset starts from the Tier~A+B primary catalogue and
applies the observational Gold cut before comparing point-estimate orbit
summaries.  The Gold cut barely changes the orbit distribution: median
$\Rperi$ is 114.8\,pc in the Gold subset versus 112.5\,pc in the full
Tier~A+B catalogue, and all Gold stars have finite action solutions in
the adopted static potential (Table~\ref{tab:gold_dynamical}).  The subset is intended for individual-star
follow-up rather than for redefining the full catalogue.

\input{tables/v15/tab_gold_dynamical.tex}

\paragraph{Threshold sensitivity within the current local sample.}
The 25\kms threshold is a practical operating cut, not a special physical
boundary.  The candidate pool is therefore released with
per-star $P(\vgrf<25\kms)$ values and tier labels rather than only a hard
threshold flag; alternative threshold cuts can be reconstructed directly
from the machine-readable catalogue.

\input{tables/v15/tab_threshold.tex}

\paragraph{Velocity-threshold Monte Carlo propagation.}
Adaptive Monte Carlo propagation was run for all 20{,}829
near-threshold candidates.  The baseline pass used 500 realisations per
source, with 5{,}000-realisation refinement in the transition region and
10{,}000-realisation refinement near the hard 25\kms point-estimate
boundary.  The resulting tier labels, rather than a single point
estimate, define the catalogue (Table~\ref{tab:mc}).

\input{tables/v15/tab_mc.tex}

\paragraph{Halo-mass sensitivity on the full Tier~A+B+C catalogue.}
A full-sample halo-mass sensitivity study was repeated for the Tier~A+B+C catalogue, bracketing recent Milky Way mass estimates
\citep[e.g.][]{Cautun2020,Wang2020}.  Varying the halo mass by $\pm15\%$ changes the
 median point-estimate cylindrical pericentre from 116\,pc in the matched 40{,}001-point
default integration to 119\,pc and 111\,pc, respectively, and leaves the
long-apocentre inner-reach count unchanged at three.  The high-eccentricity result is
therefore driven primarily by the observed phase-space configuration:
all 621 Tier~A+B+C stars have $e>0.8$ in the default static
potential.

\paragraph{Bar-perturbation sensitivity.}
Because many catalogue stars reach the inner Galaxy, we integrate a barred
Hunter/Sormani variant \citep{Sormani2022} over the pattern-speed grid
$\Omega_p=24$, 28, 33, 37.5, $41\kms\kpc^{-1}$, bracketing the slower
$\Omega_p\approx24\pm3\kms\kpc^{-1}$ bar of \citet{Horta2025} and the
commonly adopted $35$--$45\kms\kpc^{-1}$ range
\citep{Clarke2019,Zhang2024LPV}.  Barred integration leaves the qualitative
low-\vgrf{} radiality intact but raises the long-apocentre inner-reach
count from three (static) to 21 (barred default), spanning 7--31 across the
grid, with the bar-angle and halo-flattening ranges given in
Tables~\ref{tab:potential} and~\ref{tab:sensitivity}.  The barred
 \emph{median} cylindrical pericentre is itself sampling-sensitive (20\,pc to 14\,pc
over 20{,}001--40{,}001 trajectory points), so we rely on threshold counts
rather than barred medians for inner-reach comparisons and retain the
barred integrations as systematic tests rather than catalogue values.

Because the bar can induce chaos on the $\Rperi$ scales sampled here
\citep{Dillamore2023,Dillamore2024,WoudenbergHelmi2025}, we computed
Agama's normalized finite-time Lyapunov indicator $\lambda T_{\rm orb}$ at
$\Omega_p=37.5\kms\kpc^{-1}$ for the three static-potential inner-reach
candidates, the ten Sgr~A* approacher seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:sgrA_candidates}), and a random 100-star Tier~A control
sample.  Bar-induced chaos is present but bounded: none of the three reach
candidates, one of ten seeds, and four of 100 controls reach the
order-unity strongly-chaotic regime, and the closest-approach seeds are not
separated from the controls by the chaos indicator
(Fig.~\ref{fig:chaos_rperi}).

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_chaos_vs_rperi.pdf}
\caption{Finite-time Lyapunov indicator in the default barred
Hunter/Sormani potential at $\Omega_p=37.5\kms\kpc^{-1}$, plotted

Agama as a practical strongly chaotic threshold.}
\label{fig:chaos_rperi}
\end{figure}

As a second-potential check, re-integrating the catalogue in the
made-to-measure \citet{Portail2017} bar---an independent disc and flattened
Einasto dark halo, constructed via the \citet{Sormani2022} recipe at
$\Omega_p=37.5\kms\kpc^{-1}$ and a $25^\circ$ bar angle---gives
$N_{\rm reach}=15^{+5.0}_{-3.8}$ (\citealt{Gehrels1986} 68\%) and the same
high-eccentricity character, overlapping the barred Hunter+2024 value
$21^{+5.7}_{-4.5}$ within Gehrels~$1\sigma$ (Table~\ref{tab:reach}).  The
inner-reach result is therefore robust to swapping the disc/halo background
at fixed bar parameterisation; the stricter 3D Sgr~A* closest-approach
probabilities are treated separately in Appendix~\ref{app:sgrA}.

\paragraph{Radial-velocity uncertainty robustness.}
As an additional defensive cut, we required Gaia DR3 radial-velocity
uncertainty $\sigma_{RV} < 2$\kms{} on top of the Gold filter.  This
removes the noisier RVS solutions and retains 171 of the 420 Gold stars
($40.7^{+2.4}_{-2.4}\%$).  The comparatively aggressive reduction
reflects that DR3 RVS uncertainties are intrinsically larger than
2\kms{} for many of the cool giants that dominate the sample at the
typical distance, where the magnitude-dependent inflation of $\sigma_{RV}$
\citep{Katz2023} interacts with the $\sim 25\kms$ velocity scale of
interest.  Within the surviving subset the median eccentricity remains
high ($0.968$ vs.\ $0.971$ for the full Gold sample), the median
\vgrf{} is similar ($16.4$\kms{} vs.\ $15.8$\kms), and 100\% retain
$e > 0.8$.  The high-eccentricity claim survives this additional quality
cut.

\paragraph{Solar-parameter sensitivity.}
Table~\ref{tab:solar_variants} brackets the Solar-parameter dependence:
across the literature range of $R_0$, $V_c$, and the LSR motion, tier
counts on the legacy preselection subsample vary by $\sim50\%$, while
the ensemble orbital geometry is stable to $\sim12\%$ in $\Rperi$ and
$\sim1\%$ in $\Rapo$ and eccentricity.

\section{Inner-Galaxy Close Approaches} \label{app:sgrA}

 The most extreme central-passage claims require additional caution.  The
 orbit integrations are fully three-dimensional; only the separately
 reported $\Rperi$ summary is cylindrical.  In
the point-estimate products, the closest static and barred
trajectories reach within 3.1 and 4.3\,pc of the Galactic centre,
respectively.  Dedicated 5,000-realisation Monte Carlo propagation of
the closest static and barred seeds shows that no candidate has
$P(r_{\rm sph,min}<10\,{\rm pc})>0.5$, while six candidates have
$P(r_{\rm sph,min}<100\,{\rm pc})>0.5$.  These probabilities use the
spherical Galactocentric closest approach,
$r_{\rm sph}=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$, and are Monte Carlo posterior
probability counts, not the point-estimate cylindrical $\Rperi$ counts
above.  Since $r_{\rm sph}\ge \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, the Sgr~A* set is a
 stricter subset of the cylindrical spin-axis-reach set.  We therefore
describe these as candidate inner-Galaxy approachers, not as a confirmed
population of Sgr~A*-passing stars.  A three-point quadratic
interpolation of $r_{\rm sph}^2(t)$ around each sampled trajectory
minimum is included in the probabilities in Table~\ref{tab:sgrA_candidates}.
Figure~\ref{fig:sgrA_cdfs} shows the corresponding cumulative
probability curves for the 20 closest point-estimate seeds in the static
and barred-default integrations.

\section{Action-Space and Bar Resonances} \label{app:resonance}

The same $L_z$ axis is the natural place to read off where the
catalogue's angular-momentum distribution sits relative to the bar
resonances.  Figure~\ref{fig:resonances} plots the Tier~A+B+C catalogue
in $L_z$--$J_R$ St\"ackel-fudge action space and overlays the inner
Lindblad, corotation, and outer Lindblad loci derived from the
axisymmetrised Hunter+2024 potential for the full pattern-speed grid
$\Omega_p\in\{24,28,33,37.5,41\}\kms\kpc^{-1}$.  The Tier~A points
concentrate near $L_z\approx0$ at large $J_R$, intersecting the ILR loci
of several pattern speeds rather than piling up at any single corotation
or OLR; this is the same low-angular-momentum, high-radial-action
signature expected of compact-pericentre orbits, and it is consistent
with the matched-control OLR-fraction check in
Section~\ref{sec:band_comparison}.

We stress that this overlay is qualitative.  The St\"ackel-fudge actions
on which it is built carry a large vertical-action uncertainty for these
highly eccentric, inner-plunging orbits---the median absolute fractional
$J_z$ difference between catalogue and orbit-time-averaged actions is
$85\%$ in Tier~A and $62\%$ over the full Tier~A+B+C sample, against
$\lesssim10\%$ for $J_R$ and $L_z$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity})---so
the figure should be read as
locating the catalogue's low-$L_z$, high-$J_R$ concentration relative to
the resonance loci, not as a quantitative resonance-occupancy
classification.  The weighted matched-control comparison does not favour a
simple outer-Lindblad pile-up explanation.

\section{Present-Day Clustering Null Test} \label{app:dbscan}

As a final present-day compactness check, we searched the slow catalogue
for open-cluster- or association-like aggregates in 3D Galactocentric
position (Table~\ref{tab:cluster_search}).  The Tier~A+B sample contains
one pair below 25\,pc, with a closest-pair separation of 20.2\,pc.  The
broader Tier~A+B+C sample contains two close pairs below 25\,pc, but a
DBSCAN sensitivity grid \citep{Ester1996,Schubert2017} over
$\varepsilon=\{5,10,15,25,50\}$\,pc and
\texttt{min\_samples}$=\{3,4,5,7,10\}$ returns zero compact groups for
both Tier~A+B and Tier~A+B+C.  Thus the catalogue is not explained by
one or more present-day bound clusters or compact associations.  This
does not rule out common accretion-related origins or action-space
substructure, which need not remain compact in present-day position and
is more naturally addressed with the integral-of-motion and action-space
methods developed for halo debris searches
\citep{HelmiDeZeeuw2000,Borsato2020,Lovdal2022,Dodd2023}.

\section{External Low-Azimuthal-Velocity Comparison} \label{app:filion}

\citet{Filion2025} provide the closest published Gaia-era comparison
sample selected by low Galactocentric azimuthal velocity rather than by
low total Galactic rest-frame speed.  Resolving their supplementary
APOGEE identifiers to Gaia DR3 source identifiers gives 69 unique Gaia
sources, all satisfying the present Gaia-RVS parent-source contract.
Under the present low-\vgrf{} diagnostic tiering, however, all 69 are
Tier~X: three enter the 20{,}829-source Monte Carlo candidate pool, but
none enter Tier~A, Tier~A+B, or Tier~A+B+C.  Across the full Filion set,
the same Gaia RVS quality audit classifies 66 sources as \texttt{ok} and
three as \texttt{poor}; three have RV-variability flags and two are
matched in the Gaia DR3 non-single-star two-body-orbit table.  The three
candidate-pool overlaps themselves are RVS-quality \texttt{ok} and
NSS-clean, although one has RUWE just above 1.4.  Thus the comparison
separates two definitions of ``slow'': Filion et al. select low
azimuthal rotation with disc-like chemistry, whereas the present
catalogue selects low total \vgrf{} independent of chemistry and applies
the same probability and quality gates to every source.
